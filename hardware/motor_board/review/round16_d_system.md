| R16D-01 | **MAJOR** | §8.1 weapon row 0 (switch open) | **Row 0 has no persistence time and is evaluated first.  Every loaded contact bounce longer than ~0.5–0.7 ms matches it, so the robot is held (weapon coast, drives off) until the operator clears it.  Row 2 and §9 step 6 exist so that this does not happen.**<br>• Row 0's evidence is "INA239 current ≈ 0 while the drum is driven".  While the switch or XT30 contact is open, RS4 carries only the C14/U13 current (~0), because Q7/Q8 are still on and BAT_IN is disconnected.<br>• In a 20 A bounce the break comes ~0.2 ms in (U2 UVLO).  The classifier then waits ≤ ~0.45 ms for a post-event bus conversion.  The latest 150 µs shunt conversion therefore lies wholly inside the open window for any bounce ≳ 0.66 ms, and it reads ≈ 0 A.  At the event the drum *was* being driven, so row 0 matches before row 2 is reached.<br>• "VBUS following the drum's BEMF" cannot be measured in 0.5 ms and does not separate the two cases.  If row 0 is instead run as a continuous monitor with no time qualifier, it fires even without a break: a 1 ms bounce while the drum holds speed at a few amps leaves the bus up on the drum's BEMF and reads 0 A.<br>• Result: a connector bounce during drum impacts, the commonest supply event in a fight, disables the whole robot until someone presses "clear", disarms and re-arms.  §9 step 6 expects "supply (or trip) class, no latch" for 0.1–3 ms interruptions, which contradicts this.<br>• **The other direction:** the "while the drum is driven" qualifier contradicts §7.5 ("pack current ≈ 0 with the bus up").  At the end of a match the drum is disarmed and coasting. With the switch closed and no regen the pack always supplies ≥ ~100 mA (calcs §9 idle drain), so "≈ 0 A" needs no drum qualifier | [D l.650, l.652, l.521, l.619, l.758–759]; calcs §9 "drain switched on and idle ~100–120 mA"; [S hotplug.out] loaded bounce floor 4.4–4.8 V; INA239 150 µs conversions [D l.616]; round15 R15D-01 (break at ~0.21 ms) | Make row 0 a **continuous monitor, not a break-classification row**: \|I_pack\| < ~20 mA with VBUS > ~8 V, sustained **≥ ~50–100 ms** (far longer than any bounce that rows 1–12 handle, and far shorter than the drum's seconds of hold-up), whether or not the drum is driven. Row 6 accepts that impacts cause comparator trips ("impact desync").  After a hit the drum is slower.  From 50 % speed the 20 A spin-up needs ~0.23 s to get back to 90 % (spinup.out: t90 = 0.52 s from rest, near-constant torque), plus the 50–100 ms catch-spin, and full commanded speed is only reached asymptotically.  Three hits within ~1 s, each landing before the drum has fully recovered, is a normal drum-spinner exchange (driving back into the opponent, grinding), and that sequence matches row 5 literally: weapon latched until the operator clears it. That produces **stall cut-outs** (row 10), not comparator trips, so row 5 never counts it.  The weapon then cycles 0.5 s at 20 A into the short and 1 s off, for ever, never latching (row 10 and TH1 both "never latch"), and the operator sees only "stall" | [D l.655, l.656, l.660]; [S spinup.out] 20 A: t90 0.52 s, 25.6 k rpm; round15_a R15A-04 (FOC at standstill regulates a phase–phase short as load; its fix: a speed threshold, counting stall cut-outs too) | Replace "before reaching the commanded speed" with "**without the drum ever exceeding a low speed threshold** (e.g. the observer's minimum lock speed, ~10–15 % of top speed)", and count stall cut-outs (row 10) as well as comparator trips toward it. Better, and cheap: at every restart from standstill, first run a short three-vector test pulse (a few tens of µs each, low duty) and compare the phase-current response with the motor's measured R/L from §9.  A phase–phase short shows current in only two shunts at a much higher di/dt: latch on it directly, before any 20 A regulation.  §9 step 6: three hits in ~1 s on a heavy target → no latch |
| R16D-03 | MINOR | §8.1 drive rows 2–4 and SPI health: a single bad read | **One corrupted or missing DRV8316 read can latch both drives until the operator clears it, and nothing tells a bus-wide SPI fault apart from rows 3/4.**<br>• The DRV8316 SDO word has no parity or CRC [DRV16 Table 8-10]. At 100 Hz per chip for a 3-minute match there are ~18 000 reads per chip.<br>• Row 2 ("register mismatch") and row 4 ("garbage") cannot be told apart from one read.  R15D-N1 asked for a confirming second read before acting on a mismatch; it is not in the text.<br>• A transient SPI3-wide fault (all three devices failing) should give the "SPI health" retrying hold. | R16D-04 | MINOR | §8 timer inputs: two-step alignment with the sensor cables swapped | **If J2 and J3 are swapped at a pit repair (identical SH 6-pin cables) and both drives align at the same time, each drive's sign/motion check passes on the *other* motor's encoder.**<br>• Both rotors are pulled to the same +90°/0° electrical vectors, so each encoder sees exactly 90° electrical of motion with the correct sign (identical motors and MT6701 programming). ## 3. Checked and found sound

* **§1 / §5 margins against the spice outputs:**
  * DRV8316 VM: loaded bounce 0.80–2.26 V/µs, 2.66 V/µs at C1's −40 °C limit, quick re-close ≤ 2.09 V/µs, closure 0.002–0.005 V/µs, all against 4 V/µs [S hotplug.out].
  * Q7: 16.5 W / 22.0 W peak, 53.8–56.9 mJ.
  * Reversed pack: 13 A with D10.
  * ARM: 2.50–2.95 V, 7–10 edges, disarm 52–164 ms [S arm.out].
  * Spin-up: 22.1 A pack peak, 14.06 V minimum bus [S spinup.out].
  * Weapon bridge at 60 mA / 6 nH: VDS 29.4 V, SHx −4.58 V [S bridge.out].
  * D1 clamp 32.4 V against 40 V (DRV8316) and 35 V (C1).
  * INA239 SOVL 0x76C0 = 38.0 A, BOVL 0x17C0 = 19.0 V, SHUNT_CAL 4096 for 1.25 mA/LSB at ADCRANGE = 1 (recomputed).
  * CTRL4 0x0C90 / 0x0D10: even parity (recomputed).
* **Loss of +3V3 or a sagging +3V3** leaves every bridge off:
  * nSLEEP on +3V3 puts the DRV8316s to sleep.
  * W_EN and U6 lose their drive, so the weapon floats.
  * At ~2.5 V (MCU held in BOR) R50 still holds DRV_OFF high.
* **No operator-less re-arm into a latch.**  Every automatic restart stays inside a continuous ARM.  A W_ARM_S fall always needs a new edge plus throttle zero.  Latch clearing is operator-only (§3.5 and §8.1 consistent).
* **No reversed drive from a phase swap or an encoder A/B swap:** the alignment sign check latches the drive.  The J2/J3 swap is the exception (R16D-04).
* **Row ordering for the shared W_nFAULT** (DIAG_ALRT before the timing rows) and the post-event evidence window (R15D-01/02) are now consistent.

**Verdict: 0 BLOCKER, 2 MAJOR, 2 MINOR, 7 NOTE.**  The hardware and the §1 margins are clean.  The two MAJORs are
round-15 text in §8.1 that, taken literally, turns the two commonest fight events (a connector bounce with the drum
driven, a flurry of drum hits) into operator-clear holds.  Row 5 also misses the no-trip standstill short it was
written for.  All four findings are firmware-contract text fixes; no hardware change is needed.
