| R13D-01 | **MINOR** | §8 fault classes: supply class vs a single failed drive | **Device-level indicators count as "supply class" on their own.  So one dead or unreachable DRV8316 creates an endless restart loop that nothing counts, and the shared DRV_OFF drags the healthy drive into it.**<br><br>The supply-class list is "any break or fault that coincides with VBAT < ~9 V, U2 UVLO, an INA239 SOVL alert during a bus recharge, **DRV8316 CP-UV or NPOR = 0**" [D l.599].  As written, NPOR = 0 or CP-UV by itself qualifies, with no evidence from the bus.  Supply-class events are "not counted toward any latch", and they restart once VBAT has been above 12 V for 20 ms.<br><br>Now take the design's own intended survivable failure: "if a DRV8316 fails shorted, R302/R402 act as its fuse" [D l.581].<br>• With R402 open, U4 has no VM.  Its SDO is not driven, and MISO has the PC11 pull-down [D l.258], so every 100 Hz read-back returns 0x0000.  That reads as NPOR = 0, and all CTRL registers mismatch.  The parity of an all-zero word is even, so it passes.<br>• The action on NPOR = 0 or a mismatch is "DRV_OFF high, rewrite the sequence" [D l.605].  DRV_OFF is **shared** by both drives [D l.214].<br>• The supply-class restart then fires at once, because VBAT is normal.<br>• Result: every ~10 ms poll, both drives coast, U4 is rewritten, and they restart.  This repeats forever, uncounted and never latched. After a reset the chip is in 6x mode, where INH = INL = 1 is Hi-Z; a chip with no VM is safe in any state. | R13D-02 | **MINOR** | §8 start angle: d-axis alignment and "Z is the reference" | **The round-12 start-angle procedure can leave a wrong angle in place, and the Z check never corrects it.**<br><br>**(a) The Z check is only relative.**  "Store the count at the first Z; at each later Z compare" [D l.607].  That catches lost counts.  It does not catch an alignment error, because the first Z inherits whatever offset the alignment left.  The MT6701 Z sits at a fixed, programmed mechanical position (ZERO register, MT6701 §7.3 / reg 0x32–0x33).  So its electrical offset is a constant of the build, but the contract never uses it.<br><br>**(b) Alignment has a dead band.**<br>• A rotor near 180° from the injected vector feels ~zero torque.  With friction T_f it stays wherever \|sin(err)\|·K_t·I < T_f.<br>• Example: K_t·I = 2.73 mN·m at 1 A.  Skid friction of ~0.6 N on the robot, reflected through the 21.6 mm wheel and 28.5:1 at 80 %, is ≈ 0.57 mN·m at the motor.  That gives a dead band of ±asin(0.21) ≈ **±12°** around the 180° point (~7 % of start positions).  There the stored angle is ~180° off, and FOC drives the wheel **backwards** at low speed.  The plausibility check only acts "above a few thousand rpm".<br>• A wheel pinned against a wall or by an opponent (typical after the hit that caused a reset) makes T_f ≥ K_t·I.  Then the error can be anything.<br><br>**(c) Alignment is required "at every drive enable after a reset", even while the rotor is turning.**  After a brownout or IWDG reset mid-match, the compute board stays up on its 470 µF hold-up and keeps commanding [D l.359]. Z is then a true reference, and any alignment error lasts at most one motor rev (≈ 4.8 mm of wheel travel).<br>Until the first Z:<br>• align only when the encoder shows no motion over ~20 ms;<br>• if it is moving, wait for Z (≤ 1.3 ms at 46 k rpm) or catch with the observer;<br>• align in **two steps** (e.g. 90° then 0° electrical) and check that the encoder moved in the expected direction by about the expected count. ## 2. Checked and found sound (no finding)

**Can the supply-class auto-restart mask a real short?**  Not through the bus-voltage path.

* A weapon short lasts ≤ 4 µs (VDS) or ≤ 1 µs (comparator).  Neither the 0.87 ms VBAT_SNS nor a 150 µs INA239 VBUS conversion sees it as < 9 V.
* The recharge that follows is ≈ 1–2 mC.  That is ≈ 7–13 A averaged over a conversion, well short of SOVL on its own.
* U2 and DRV8316 UVLO are not reached by one event (R13D-N1a).
* The masking risk that remains is the device-level path (R13D-01) and the missing precedence rule (N1).

**Restart loops that can damage hardware.**

* **Single-comparator-trip restarts** are bounded: the second trip within ~1 s latches.
* **Supply-class restarts** need the bus to have recovered.  Each loaded bounce stays ≤ 2.26 V/µs at the DRV8316 VM [S hotplug.out].  A chattering connector therefore repeats a within-limits event.  That costs availability but damages nothing.
* **A weak CP with the high side under-enhanced** trips the VDS OCP (0.13 V ≙ 6.5 mΩ at 20 A) before the FET dissipates dangerously.  That is short class.

**Round-12 items verified.**

* Alignment travel: 0.40 mm (½ electrical rev at 6 pole pairs, 28.5:1, 43.2 mm wheel).
* Encoder speed cap: 50 k rpm × 4096 counts/rev = 3.4 M edges/s, 293 ns apart.  The ICxF ≤ 0b0011 filter (8 × 5.9 ns = 47 ns) passes it.
* MT6701 ABZ: no output for 50 ms after power-up (§7.3), so the "≥ 20 ms without edges" re-power rule is consistent.
* A hysteresis of 0.088° (~1 count) and the direction-dependent Z edge both fit the "a few counts" tolerance.
* R302 force test: 1 A × 0.1 Ω = 0.10 V (confirmed by round 13 B).
* Armed soak and full-pack regen test: sound.

**Other system modes re-checked, with no new issue.**

* **Hot-plug and bounce:** hotplug.out re-read.  Worst 2.66 V/µs at C1's −40 °C limit.
* **Reversed pack:** D10 in place.
* **Compute-board reset:** ARM decays in 30–200 ms; command timeout 100–250 ms.
* **Header unplugged:** R18 pull-down on W_ARM_CLK; R16/R17 pull-ups on NRST/MB_RX.
* **Sensor VS short:** handled by the TPS22945.
* **Drive lead shorts:** DRV8316 OCP latched, then counted.
* **Weapon lead shorts:** comparator or VDS trip.
* **Regen with the pack disconnected:** D1 clamp + BOVL.
* **Logic hold-up through a 0.8 ms bounce:** +5V sags ≈ 0.57 A × 0.8 ms / ~490 µF ≈ 0.9 V.  +3V3 holds, and nSHDN is filtered at 4.5 ms.
* **§1 margins:** unchanged since round 12 (VM ≤ 2.75 V/µs vs 4; 450 mA for the compute board vs ~400 mA expected; weapon VDS ≤ 29 V vs 40 V).

## 3. Summary

| ID | Sev | One line |
|---|---|---|
| R13D-01 | MINOR | NPOR/CP-UV/read-back mismatch count as supply class without bus evidence.  One dead DRV8316 (e.g. R302 fused, as the design intends) gives an uncounted endless rewrite loop.  The loop cycles the shared DRV_OFF, so it takes the healthy drive with it.  Require a VBUS dip or both devices together; count single-device events; define the single-drive latch |
| R13D-02 | MINOR | "Z is the reference" is only relative, so an alignment error (180° dead band, pinned wheel) persists and can reverse torque at low speed.  Aligning after a reset while the robot still rolls causes OCP / latching.  Calibrate the Z electrical offset once; align in two steps with a motion check; skip alignment if the encoder moves |
| R13D-03 | MINOR | A lost encoder at low speed or standstill is never detected: the drive holds a locked wheel.  Add a no-edges-under-torque test that falls back to sensorless |
| R13D-N1 | NOTE | Fault classes: state that the short class takes precedence (supply-indicator margins 2.1 µs vs a 3 µs deglitch at the aged-C1 corner).  Classify GDF, OTSD, BOVL, SOVL-without-recharge and the stall cut-out |
| R13D-N2 | NOTE | Latches are lost on an MCU reset, and the compute board re-arms automatically.  Keep latches in the backup registers; no auto re-arm of a latched weapon |
| R13D-N3 | NOTE | R44/6x boot check: an 8.7 µs divider plus sampling outside the pulse → a low-duty pulse can read 0.03 V on a 6x board.  Use a ≥ 30 µs one-shot with a software ADC sample and a drum-stopped precondition; state the failure action |

**Verdict: 0 BLOCKER, 0 MAJOR, 3 MINOR, 3 NOTE.**  Every finding is firmware-contract or bring-up text.  No
hardware change is needed, and the hardware remains ready for schematic capture.
