| R17A-01 | **MAJOR** | [D] §8.1 weapon row 5; §9 step 6 | **The restart resistance check runs up to ~1 k rpm, where the drum's back-EMF is larger than the test voltage.  The check can then latch the weapon on a healthy motor after a hit, or pass a real short.**<br><br>• The weapon motor is 1800 KV, R ≈ 0.10 Ω ([S] l.9, 31).  A 5 A DC pulse needs V = I·R ≈ 0.5 V (+ ~10 mΩ of FETs, shunt and leads).<br>• Line-line back-EMF ≈ rpm/KV: **0.56 V at 1 k rpm**, 0.28 V at 500 rpm, 0.06 V at 100 rpm.  The electrical frequency at 1 k rpm is 117 Hz (7 pole pairs), so over the 1 ms pulse the BEMF is roughly a constant, unknown offset on that pair.<br>• The current loop holds 5 A, so the applied voltage = I·R + e.  Apparent V/I = R ± e/I = 0.10 ± 0.11 Ω at 1 k rpm, ± 0.056 Ω at 500 rpm.  "Below half the bring-up value" (< ~0.055 Ω) is then met by a **healthy** motor whenever e opposes the current.  That is a weapon latch that needs an operator clear.  The same error can lift a real short above the threshold.<br>• When does it happen?  A restart from < 1 k rpm but not stopped is common: an impact drags the drum down, row 6 trips, and the catch-spin restart comes 50–100 ms later with the drum still turning slowly.<br>• **Secondary: the offset from computing V from duty.**  The pulse is ~1.5–3 % duty at 24 kHz, 0.4–1.2 µs of high-side on-time.  The 100 ns digital dead time plus the V_GS handshake ([DRV23] §8.3.1.4 p.37–38; tPD 150 ns) removes ~150–300 ns per period.  That is ~60–120 mV at 16.8 V, or +12–24 mΩ apparent at 5 A.  A dead short then reads ~20–35 mΩ against ~120–135 mΩ healthy.  At R = 0.10 Ω the contrast holds, but it is gone for a winding below ~40 mΩ, and the offset scales with VBUS.<br>• **Reference value:** §9 step 6 stores the bring-up values in the "motor without drum" step.  A bare 2822 rotor (~3e-6 kg·m²) is accelerated by 5 A × 5.3 mN·m/A to ~9 rad/s within 1 ms. "< ~10 % of the expected motion" separates swapped cables from vibration |
| N-04 | NOTE | The CTRL4 bit and the DRVOFF pin do different things.  The pin section ([DRV16] §8.4.2 p.53) says the **pin** disables driver faults such as OCP while high and may pull nFAULT low.  The register bit has only the one-line "Hi-Z FETs" description, so it is not documented whether OCP detection stays armed while the bit is set.  That does not matter while the FETs are off, and the §8.1 bench-verify (Hi-Z at speed, nFAULT behaviour) is the right test.  Add to it: after 0x0D10, confirm that the chip drives again and that IC_STAT shows no stale fault |

## Independent re-checks (no finding)

* The INA239 shunt filter (R2/R3 10 Ω, C2 100 nF) against RDIFF 92 kΩ: 0.02 % gain error, and it is systematic.
* SHUNT_CAL 0x1000, SOVL 0x76C0 = 38 A, BOVL 0x17C0 = 19.0 V: unchanged since round 16 and still correct.  A reset INA239 reverts CONFIG.ADCRANGE to 0.  SOVL would then mean 152 A, so reading back CONFIG is essential; §8.1 now lists it.
* Row 0 when the switch opens with the drum stopped: the bus falls from 16.8 V to the 9.2 V buck cutoff in ~35 ms (374 µF, ~1.1 W).  That is before the 100 ms persistence, so the board simply powers down.  The latch word goes with it, which is correct.  It is also slower than 5 V/ms, so it is not a row 2 bus event.
* Row 0 with a drum coasting while BEMF ≈ VBUS: the diode-rectified current changes by ~20 A per volt of BEMF, so the ±30 mA band spans ~1.5 mV of BEMF.  A coasting drum crosses that in milliseconds, far short of 100 ms.
* Row 5 current sensing: at the TIM1 peak sample (low sides on) both shunts of the pair carry the full 5 A.  At 40 mV/A that is 200 mV, ~250 LSB, and the valley sample of symmetric PWM gives the average current.  A 5 A × 3 × 1 ms pulse train dissipates ~7.5 mJ in the winding: negligible.
