# Review round 4 / D: system-level adversarial review (rev D)

Reviewer role: power electronics and combat robotics. This review looks for failures that cross
block boundaries. Other round-4 reviewers cover pin-level detail, the BOM and MCU timing.

Inputs: DESIGN.md rev D, design/calcs.md, design/nets.md, design/bom.csv, BOM.md, spice/*.out,
review/CHANGES.md, review/round3_e_system.md, and the datasheets SN74LVC08A (TI), BAT54S
(Nexperia) and LM74502 (TI). No design file was edited.

| **D4-01** | **MINOR** | Dynamic ARM, hung compute board | **A W_ARM_CLK frozen *high* takes ~1.1 s τ to disarm, not ~130 ms.** A hung RP2040 freezes its GPIO, so the pin is high about half the time. With CLK held high, the C15 plate at CLK is fixed at 3.3 V, and C15 discharges into W_ARM through D9's rectifier diode, which stays forward-biased. R41 therefore bleeds C15 **and** C16 in parallel: τ = 1 MΩ × (1 µF + 0.1 µF) = **1.1 s**. The typical LVC threshold (~1.4 V) is crossed after 1.1·ln(2.9/1.4) ≈ **0.8 s**, and VIL 0.8 V after 1.1·ln(2.9/0.8) ≈ **1.4 s**. The ~130 ms figure (R41·C16·ln(2.9/0.8) = 129 ms) holds only for stuck-low and unplugged. The §9 step 3 test ("holding W_ARM_CLK high … below 0.8 V within ~150 ms") will fail on a correctly built board. Safety impact is limited: the STM32 command timeout (100–250 ms) stops the weapon first, and 1.4 s is far inside the 60 s rules. But the independent hardware path is 10× slower than documented. | [D §3.2] "~130 ms"; [D §9] step 3; [N] W_ARM_CLK → C15 → ARM_AC → D9 → W_ARM, with R41 1 M and C16 100 nF; [B] C15 1 µF | Add **100 kΩ from ARM_AC to GND** (existing 0402 line). When CLK sticks high, ARM_AC then falls with τ = 100 ms, faster than W_ARM, so D9 reverse-biases and W_ARM decays at R41·C16 again: ≤ ~150 ms for high, low or open. Pumping cost: 30 µA for 1 ms per cycle, i.e. 30 mV of C15 droop. Alternative: C15 → 100 nF (τ 0.2 s, ~0.26 s). Re-derive the numbers in §3.2 and §9 |
| **D4-02** | **MINOR** | Dynamic ARM, logic interface | **W_ARM is a slow RC ramp into a non-Schmitt 74LVC08, and R41 = 1 MΩ leaves almost no leakage budget.** (1) The SN74LVC08A's input transition rate must be ≤ **8 ns/V**. W_ARM decays at ~15 V/s (τ 100 ms), about 10⁷× slower, and spends ~90 ms between 2.0 and 0.8 V. A CMOS input without hysteresis can let its output chatter while the input sits at the threshold. Each of U6's three gates crosses at its own threshold, so INLA/B/C can toggle for ~1 ms each during a disarm while TIM1 may still be driving. This is new in rev D: in rev C W_ARM was driven with fast edges. PD2 (Schmitt) is fine, but its EXTI can fire more than once on a slow edge, and the "fresh low→high edge" rule depends on that input. (2) The guaranteed input leakage is ±5 µA per LVC input at 85 °C (±20 µA at 125 °C), plus PD2's ±0.15 µA. Four inputs give 15 µA worst case, while 1 MΩ needs < 0.8 µA to reach VIL. By datasheet limits, a stuck-low CLK is not guaranteed to disarm. Real leakage is nA, so this is a guarantee gap, not an expected failure; D9's Schottky leakage pulls the right way. | [DS SN74LVC08A] Δt/Δv 8 ns/V, II ±5/±20 µA; [N] W_ARM → U6.2/5/10 and PD2; [D §8] W_ARM EXTI both edges | Put a Schmitt buffer between C16 and U6: SN74LVC1G17, a new line, or change U6 to a Schmitt-input gate. Then R41 → 100 k and C16 → 1 µF (τ unchanged at 100 ms, leakage budget 8 µA; with C15 1 µF the pump still gives ~2.85 V and ~10 ms rise). Firmware: debounce PD2 by requiring W_ARM low for ≥ 20 ms, then high for ≥ 5 ms, before an edge counts as "fresh" |
| **D4-03** | **MINOR** | Motor-MCU hang | **The contract has no independent watchdog (IWDG), and nothing stops the drives if the STM32 hangs.** On a core hang (HardFault loop, stuck ISR), TIM1, TIM8 and TIM20 keep outputting their last compare values, and DRV_OFF stays low. **Weapon:** the Pico loses the heartbeat and stops toggling, and U6 forces INL low after 0.13–1.4 s (D4-01). Until then the bridge holds a fixed voltage vector against a rotor at 25 k rpm: phase current is limited only by VDS OCP (62–93 A, 4 µs, auto-retry every 4 ms), and the INA239 SOVL sees pack current, not phase current. **Drives:** no hardware path stops them. A frozen vector locks and brakes the wheels (safe direction), or trips the DRV8316 16 A latched OCP. The robot is not driven, but it is not in a defined coast either, and it stays that way until power-off. The compute board holds NRST (J1 pin 8), but §3.5 does not require it to use it. | grep of DESIGN/calcs/CHANGES for IWDG or watchdog: none. [D §3.5] items 4–5; [D §8] command timeout (firmware only); [C §6] VDS OCP 62–93 A | [D §8]: enable the **IWDG** at boot (LSI; e.g. 20–50 ms), refreshed only when both the FOC ISR and the main loop have run (a flag in the ISR, cleared by the main loop). A reset gives W_EN low, DRV_OFF high and INL low: a full coast within ~1 ms. Report IWDGRSTF in the heartbeat. [D §3.5]: on heartbeat loss > 50 ms, the compute board stops W_ARM_CLK **and pulses NRST** |
| **D4-04** | **MINOR** | 48 kHz drives, ADC timing (firmware only) | **The documented ADC trigger cannot serve both the 48 kHz drives and the 24 kHz weapon.** In centre-aligned mode the two TIM1_CC4 matches happen at t = c and t = T − c, symmetric about TIM1's peak at T/2. TIM8/TIM20 are reset in phase by TIM1's UEV (at 0 and T/2). Their low-side-on centres (PWM mode 1: output low around the counter top) are at T/4 and 3T/4, which forces c = T/4. The weapon's low-side centre is at T/2, and neither CC4 match lands there. At t = T/4 (TIM1 CNT = ARR/2), a weapon phase's low side is on only if its duty is < 50 %. So 3-shunt weapon current sampling works for about half of each electrical cycle. Related: the "duty ≤ ~88 %" limit is a 24 kHz figure. At 48 kHz (20.8 µs period), sampling ≥ 1 µs + DLY_TARGET 1.2 µs after the low-side turn-on with the centre trigger needs a half-window ≥ 2.2 µs, so duty ≤ **~79 %** if every phase must be sampled. | [D §3.4] "TIM1_CC4 … both the up- and down-count match … the weapon uses the one centred in its low-side-on interval"; [D §8] DRV8316 row "duty ≤ ~88 %" | Firmware contract change; no hardware change. Trigger the injected groups on **TIM1_TRGO (MMS = update, RCR = 0 → UEV at both bottom and top)**. That is the same signal that resets TIM8/TIM20 through ITR0. Run the drives in **PWM mode 2**, so their low-side centre is at their counter zero, which is the trigger. The weapon, in mode 1, uses the trigger at its top (T/2). Keep TRGO2 for the regular groups. For the drives, reconstruct the highest-duty phase from the other two (standard 3-shunt practice), so the duty limit applies only to the middle phase |
| **D4-05** | **MINOR** | Shorted bus: FET, MLCC or wiring | **The bus has no over-current disconnect, and rev D's switch hiccups into a short instead of holding off. This behaviour is not documented.** *Hard short* (a double-failed leg, or a flex-cracked VBAT MLCC with a low-resistance crack): ~280 A pulls BAT_IN to ≈ 280 A × ~5 mΩ ≈ 1.4 V < the 6.5 V EN/UVLO, so U13 opens the gate in ~2 µs (tUVLO_OFF). BAT_IN then recovers at once, the gate re-ramps through C13 at 2.7 V/ms, and Q7 drives the short in its **linear region**: ~16 V across it while the current rises to the ~170 A that drags BAT_IN back below the UVLO. That is roughly 1 kW for ~0.5 ms, repeated every ~1 ms, far outside a 5×6 FET's single-pulse SOA. Q7 is likely to fail short, and the pack then feeds the fault directly. *Partial short* (0.2–0.5 Ω, e.g. a cracked 1206 MLCC): 35–80 A with BAT_IN above the UVLO, so the switch stays on. The INA239 SOVL only breaks the weapon, and the part burns. The only disconnect is the external screw switch. §6.10 acknowledges "an unfused LiPo", but §7 does not say that the electronic switch provides **no** short-circuit protection or that it hiccups. | [DS LM74502] EN/UVLO falling 1.03–1.24 V, tUVLO_OFF 2 µs, no current sense; [C §9] UVLO 6.5 V; [D §3.1]; [N] INA ALERT → W_nFAULT only | At minimum, add a §7 residual: "a bus short is interrupted only by the external switch; U13 hiccups and Q7 will likely fail; stake/orient the MLCCs (done) and strain-relieve the pads".
