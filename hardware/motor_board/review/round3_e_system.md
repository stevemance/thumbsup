| **E3-01** | **MAJOR** | Loss of the compute board; failsafe | **The motor MCU has no command-loss failsafe.** The only heartbeat in the contract runs motor → compute ([D §3.5] item 4, [D §8] USART1 row). Nothing says what the STM32 does when commands *stop* or arrive corrupted. Three ordinary events therefore leave motors running on the last command. (a) **Header knocked loose** (see E3-02): W_ARM drops through R41, so the weapon coasts, but the drives keep executing the last speed/torque set-point indefinitely. (b) **Pico hangs**: a hung RP2040 keeps its GPIO states, so **W_ARM stays high** and the weapon also keeps its last command. The "two independent processors" weapon interlock ([D §2]: ARM **and** STM32 firmware) collapses to one point, the Pico, because W_ARM is a static level. (c) **UART garbage**: no framing/CRC requirement is written, so a corrupted frame can be taken as a command. | SPARC 6.4.1: radio systems must "stop all motion in the robot (drive and weapons) when the transmitter loses power or signal"; "Robots 1 lb and less typically will be required to have drive fail-safes". NHRL: on signal loss the drive must stop within 30 s and weapons within 60 s, and a fail-safe test is part of safety inspection. [D §8] lists W_ARM EXTI handling but no receive timeout. | Add to [D §8] as hardware-contract items: (1) **command watchdog**: no valid frame for 100–250 ms → DRV_OFF high (drives coast), weapon controlled stop then CHxN low; the timeout clears only on a fresh ARM edge plus valid frames; (2) frames carry a CRC-16 and a sequence counter, and bad frames are dropped and counted; (3) on the compute board side, make W_ARM **dynamic**: a ≥ 1 kHz toggle that the Pico produces only from its main loop, detected on this board by a diode-pump RC or by timer capture on PD2, with "no toggle for > 20 ms" = disarmed. A hung Pico then disarms. Alternatively, put the physical ARM link **in series with the W_ARM wire** in hardware rather than as a Pico input. |

| **E3-04** | MINOR | Grounding; ADC integrity | **[D §6] has no board-level current-flow or ground-floorplan rule.** Pack return current (22 A spin-up, 27 A at a 25 A limit [C §7]) runs from the weapon shunts and the DRV8316 PGND through the GND planes to Q8/Q7 at the battery pads. On JLC's default 4-layer stack (0.5 oz inner, ~1 mΩ/□ at 17.5 µm) that current drops ~20–45 mV across 1–2 squares. If the MCU, U2's AGND/DVDD island, a DRV8316 AGND island or J1 sits in that path, the CSA-to-ADC reference shifts under load, and the start-up offset calibration cannot remove a shift that depends on load. The effect is 20 mV / 40 mV/A = **0.5 A weapon error**, 20 mV / 0.15 V/A = **0.13 A drive error** (13 % of the 1 A running current), plus di/dt spikes on the same path. The header GND is the compute board's only ground, so the same drop moves its ground too. It becomes a real loop if the standoffs (E3-02) are metal and grounded on both boards, or if USB is attached while a pack is connected. | [C §7] pack currents; [D §6] rules 1–9 cover the commutation loop, Kelvin sense, straps and thermal pads but not where the DC return runs; [D §3.3] AGND/PGND partition applies only at each DRV8316. | Add a layout rule 0: battery pads, RS4, Q7/Q8, C1, the weapon bridge and the drive ICs sit on one edge in the order the current flows. The MCU, U2's logic side, U5 and J1 sit on the far side, outside any path between a power stage and Q8. Specify **1 oz inner copper**. Tie J1's GND pins to the plane at the logic end only. Make the mounting holes non-plated (or plated but not connected to GND on one of the two boards) so the standoffs cannot close a second ground path. Tell the user to debug over USB with the weapon unpowered, or through an isolator. |
| **E3-05** | MINOR | Loss of the compute board; reset | **NRST (and MB_RX) sit at an undefined level whenever the RP2040 is in reset or boot ROM.** RP2040 GPIOs reset with the pad pull-down enabled (PADS_BANK0 reset value 0x56: PDE = 1), 50–80 kΩ. Each Pico reset (its watchdog, a brownout, a boot) therefore leaves the motor MCU in an undefined reset state that noise can toggle. MB_RX (PC5, TT pin with its internal pull-up) sits at the same mid-level and produces framing errors. [D §3.5] item 5 asks for "NRST open-drain only", which an RP2040 cannot meet during its own reset without extra parts. | [DS STM32G474] NRST RPU 25/40/55 kΩ, VIL(NRST) 0.3 VDD, VIH 0.7 VDD; RP2040 datasheet pad defaults (pull-down on at reset). | Add **R 10 kΩ NRST → +3V3** on this board: 3.3 × 50/(50 + 10) = 2.75 V > VIH even against the strongest RP2040 pull-down; the compute board still pulls it to ground. | **E3-06** | MINOR | Brownout ordering; restart with the drum spinning | **After an STM32 reset the weapon can re-engage without a fresh ARM.** In a sag that reaches the buck UVLO, the 5 V rail decays at ~11 V/ms (0.45 A into ~40 µF). A short event can therefore reset the STM32 while the Pico rides through with **W_ARM still high**. [D §8] re-arm needs an explicit restart only "on ARM low", and the boot order ends with "weapon TIM1 CHxN only while W_ARM (PD2) is high". A freshly booted STM32 that sees PD2 high may therefore catch the spinning drum and resume on an uninitialised command. | **E3-07** | MINOR | Partial pack / 12 V; bus budget | **No firmware bus-power budget or foldback, and three independent limits sit close to normal operating points.** (1) **UVLO**: at the end of a match a pack at 3.6 V/cell (14.4 V) with 100 mΩ (pack, lead, warm) under a 22 A spin-up plus ~8 A of drive draw sags to 14.4 − 30 × 0.1 = **11.4 V**; at 3.4 V/cell, **10.6 V**.
