## Verified (no finding)

**D1 clamp and the ~10 A limit (l.186, l.610, l.763).**

* The SMBJ20A (BORN electrical table) has VBR 22.2–24.5 V at 1 mA and VC 32.4 V at IPP 18.6 A.  That gives Rd ≈ 0.425 Ω, so 10 A clamps at 24.5 + 4.25 = 28.7 V at 25 °C.
* With the 68 k/10 k 1 % dividers (netlist: R22/R23 → W_VA → U1 pin 18 PA4; R24/R25 → PA5 pin 19; R63/R64 → PA3 pin 17), the pins see 3.68 V nominal and 3.74 V worst.  So "≤ ~3.7 V" (§8) and "≤ ~3.75 V" (§3.2) are both right.
* DS12288 Table 14 gives 4.0 V for TT_xx.  The pin table has PA3/PA4/PA5 as TT_a and PA2 as FT_a.  Table 15 note 3 says there is no positive injection below the maximum.
* The step 6 figure "4.0 V at ~12–14 A" is right: 4.0 / 0.1305 = 30.65 V, which D1 reaches at 14.5 A (worst dividers, 25 °C) and ~12–13 A hot.
* PA3 has C68 100 nF (τ 0.87 ms), so it sees even less than PA4/PA5 during a ≤ 0.5 ms clamp.  The "short (≪ ~9 µs) spike" wording for the phase-divider 1 nF is correct (8.7 µs).

**§9 step 6 regen-current check.**  D1 is on VBAT, after RS4 (netlist `VBAT,D1,1,K`).  With the switch closed, RS4 carries regen minus board load, which is what D1 would take with the switch open.

The check is conservative in two ways:

* With the switch open, the DRV8316 OVP (20–22 V) Hi-Zs the drives before D1 conducts (VBR ≥ 22.2 V), so D1 sees only the weapon's share.
* At a fixed torque, the weapon's DC current falls as the bus rises.

The < 18.3 V VBUS check is looser: 10 A × ~74 mΩ lifts a 16.8 V pack to only ~17.5 V.  So the new current criterion is the one that actually binds.

**§9 step 4 idle rule.**

* 1.6–1.9 W at 16.8 V is 95–113 mA, as stated.
* When row 0 is not suspended, the estimate is idle plus the positive terms only: negative drive power suspends row 0, and the weapon's negative term is excluded.  So the idle term only has to stay outside the band, and "band ≤ half the measured minimum" guarantees that with 2× margin.
* Measuring at 16.8 V (the constant-power minimum) is the right corner.

**Braking row "≈ 0.4 s".**  I checked it at 64 J with 10 A DC (~175 W) down to ~16 k rpm, then at the 20 A torque limit: ~0.5 s ignoring losses, and copper and switching losses shorten it.  Close enough for a text estimate.

## Findings

### R28A-01 (MINOR) — the Z-count check cannot detect a slipped or loose magnet, but l.615 now says it does

l.615: "at every later Z check the count (mod 4096): a few counts is drift (correct it); a larger mismatch (**a slipped or loose magnet or encoder**) marks the encoder suspect".

The MT6701 generates A/B *and* Z from its own measured magnet angle.  Z "代表0°的位置", i.e. Z marks the 0° position of the measured angle (MT6701 datasheet §7.3, p.12, Fig. 8/9).

* **A slipped magnet** (or a loose sensor board) rotates A/B and Z together.  The count between Z pulses stays exactly 4096 (x4 of 1024 PPR), and the count at Z stays consistent.  What changes is the relation between count and *rotor electrical angle*, and no encoder-only check sees that.  Round 27 D's own analysis says so (round27_d N1: "a slipped magnet moves Z together with A/B, so the count at Z stays consistent").
* **A rattling magnet** makes back-and-forth edges that the quadrature counter follows and that Z tracks.  That is also consistent.
* **A count mismatch** at Z comes only from edges lost or added between the chip and TIM3/TIM2: an intermittent connector or cable (the 4.7 k pull-ups freeze a line), noise past the Schmitt buffer and ICxF filter, or over-speed.

The rule itself (mismatch → encoder suspect → sensorless) is good and should stay.  But the parenthetical tells the firmware author that a mid-match magnet slip is covered by the Z check.  It is not: only the observer comparison above a few thousand rpm catches it (bounded per R27D-N1), plus the Z-vs-stored-offset test at the next alignment.

Fix (text only, l.615): replace "(a slipped or loose magnet or encoder)" with "(lost or extra edges: an intermittent sensor cable/connector, noise or over-speed)".  Optionally add "a slipped magnet keeps Z and the count consistent; only the observer check catches it".  If the parenthetical is corrected, the CHANGES.md round 27 row "(slipped magnet)" wants the same correction.

### R28A-02 (MINOR) — round 27's new bounce figures contradict two unchanged statements

Round 27 added "≤ 2.33 V/µs at 0 °C, 2.82 V/µs only at C1's −40 °C limit" to §7.2 (l.510).  Two statements it did not update now contradict it:

* **§1 Environment row (l.36):** "keep **every** simulated switch/bounce event ≤ **2.75 V/µs** even with C1 at its −40 °C ESR limit".  The reproduced −40 °C case is 2.82 V/µs, and 2.72–2.73 V/µs without the extra load, over the 0.8–1.2 ms part of the window that round 27 added.
* **§7.2 itself, next sentence (l.511–512):** "loaded bounce ≤ 2.26 V/µs with C1 at its aged 0 °C bound … and 2.66 V/µs even at its −40 °C limit".  The same paragraph now also says 2.33 and 2.82.
* Minor in the same place: "re-closes hard from ~4–10 V".  The loaded-bounce cases re-close from 3.8–4.5 V.  At 3.8 V the DRV8316s are below their 4.1–4.3 V UVLO and reset; §8.1 "drives resume" step 1 already handles that.

There is no hardware consequence: all values are < 4 V/µs, and the −40 °C column is outside the 0–50 °C environment.  But §1 is the requirement statement, and it now claims a margin the package's own sims contradict.

Fix (text only):

* l.36: "≤ ~2.35 V/µs over 0–50 °C (≤ 2.82 V/µs even with C1 at its −40 °C ESR limit)".
* l.511–512: "loaded bounce ≤ 2.33 V/µs … and 2.82 V/µs even at its −40 °C limit (0.1–1.3 ms bounces)".
* "~4–10 V" → "~4–10 V (≈ 3.8 V for a loaded bounce)".

### N1 (NOTE) — §9 step 6 regen check: say where the drives' share goes

The "(weapon and drives combined; with the switch open D1 would carry exactly this current)" is conservative, not exact.  With the switch open the drives Hi-Z at their 20–22 V OVP before D1 (≥ 22.2 V) conducts.  Passing the combined test therefore covers the weapon-only case with margin.  No change needed.  If the wording is touched again, "at most this current" is the accurate phrase.

### N2 (NOTE) — VBAT_SNS_H to the compute board during a D1 clamp

At 28.7 V the divider node is 3.7 V, and it reaches the compute-board pin through R33 100 k with the requested ~10 nF (τ ≈ 1 ms).  A ≤ 0.5 ms clamp therefore raises that pin by well under the 3.8 V RP2040 limit, and any clamp current is µA.  The J1 table's "3.3 V-safe up to 25.7 V" is a steady-state figure.  Nothing to change.  Recorded because §3.5 now has a bus level (28.7 V) above that figure.
