| R15A-01 | **MAJOR** | [D] §8.1 "Per-drive coast"; §8 DRV8316C row (read-back, "on a mismatch … rewrite the sequence"); §9 step 6 | **The 6x-mode coast goes against a datasheet instruction, brakes at speed, and is undone by the 100 Hz register check.  A per-chip coast bit already exists.**<br><br>**(1) Datasheet.**  TI: "does not recommend changing the MODE pin or PWM_MODE register during operation of the power MOSFETs. It also waits behind any SPI3 transfer in progress and is pre-empted by two 48 kHz FOC ISRs and one 24 kHz ISR (60–80 % CPU), so 20–40 µs is realistic.  At the 46.5 k rpm cap the phase BEMF is ≈ 7.7 V pk (λ ≈ 0.30 mWb, 6 pole pairs).  With L_ph 5–20 µH (unpublished), di/dt = 0.4–1.5 A/µs, so the current reaches 6–23 A in 15 µs and up to 15–60 A in 40 µs.  The OCP trips at 10–22 A (OCP_LVL 0, [DRV16] p.13) with a 0.2–1.2 µs deglitch.  The latched OCP then pulls nFAULT, and on the right drive the §8 rule "R_nFAULT EXTI → DRV_OFF high" also stops the healthy drive.  Leaving the coast ("outputs low, write 3x") has the same window.<br><br>**(3) Read-back conflict.**  While a drive is coasted its CTRL2 reads 6x (0x78).  The ~100 Hz check expects 0x7C, and on a mismatch it rewrites the sequence, which writes CTRL2 0x087C (3x) while INH is forced high.  That turns on all three high sides: a brake at speed.<br><br>**(4) The existing alternative.**  CTRL4 bit 7 **DRV_OFF: "1h = Hi-Z FETs"** ([DRV16] Figure 8-54 / Table 8-21, p.64). No brake window and no mode change.<br>• **Leave the coast:** CTRL1 0x0603 → CTRL4 0x0D10 → CTRL1 0x0606, then a catch-spin (zero-current FOC with the observer/encoder angle).<br>• Make the read-back **state-aware**: expect CTRL4 0x90 while coasted.  In any rewrite of a drive that should stay coasted, write CTRL4 0x0C90; it already comes before CTRL2 in the sequence.<br>• **Reset chip (NPOR = 0, 6x, bit cleared):** force its INH high first (Hi-Z in 6x), then rewrite with 0x0C90.<br>• **Caveats (verify on the bench, §9 step 6):** the bit is documented only in the register map (Table 5-1 calls it "DRVOFF pin configuration").  The DRVOFF pin may pull nFAULT low (§8.4.2 note, p.53), and the bit may do the same.  Mask or expect that nFAULT, so it does not trigger the R_nFAULT → DRV_OFF-high rule.<br>• If the 6x method is kept at all, its CTRL2 frame is 0x0978, and the read-back rule must expect it |
| R15A-02 | MINOR | [D] §8.1 "If that chip does not answer on SPI … take DRV_OFF high only until the read-back shows it is not driving"; §9 step 6 "hold R_nCS high → right drive latches, left resumes" | **"Read-back" cannot tell an unpowered chip from a powered chip that does not answer, and the second cannot be coasted on its own.**<br><br>Both read 0x0000 (MISO pull-down). ## Notes

| ID | Sev | Note |
|---|---|---|
| N-01 | NOTE | **RCC_CSR and reset counting on the G474** ([RM] RCC_CSR, reset chapter).  There is no POR flag.  **BORRSTF** is set at power-on and at a BOR, and the latch-word rule works if it uses BORRSTF: after a real BOR the word is still valid and is used as-is.  **PINRSTF** is set with every internal reset (the pulse generator drives NRST), so test IWDGRSTF/SFTRSTF/BORRSTF first, then clear with RMVF.  A lockup is not a reset source: lockup, HardFault (handler waits) and a plain IWDG timeout all show as IWDGRSTF.  To tell them apart, write a TAMP breadcrumb from the HardFault handler and in the CLL break path.  "Within ~2 s of a break" and "> ~3 resets within ~10 s" need uptime breadcrumbs (e.g. a "break < 2 s ago" flag cleared by a 2 s timer; a reset count cleared after 10 s of healthy uptime), because no clock survives a system reset with the RTC unused |
| N-02 | NOTE | **"Comparator flag" = the EXTI falling-edge pending bit** of COMP1/COMP3/COMP6 (EXTI lines 21/29/32, [RM] EXTI table).  With POL = 0 the output falls on a trip.  TIM1 has no per-source break flag (BIF only), and COMPx_CSR.VALUE is not latched: it returns high about 1 µs after the bridge turns off.  Enable those EXTI lines (event or pending only) whenever the comparators are enabled |
| N-03 | NOTE | **A loaded bounce with the drum at speed will often be classed "Trip", not "Supply".**  Once the bus falls below the BEMF, the FOC bridge pushes regen current into the low sides, which reads as SOx low.  Rows 1–2 come before row 3.  The outcome is benign: a restart, no latch, and no DRV_OFF (the DRV8316s handle their own UV).  The §9 step 6 expectation "supply class only" should read "no latch; Supply or Trip" |

## Independent re-checks (no finding)

* Regeneration and both sims are identical (above).  The ARM, hotplug and bounce numbers quoted in §1/§3/§5/§7 match
  the .out files.
* DRV8316 register frames in §8 have correct even parity (0x0603, 0x0606, 0x087C, 0x097D, 0x0D10 checked).
* The INA239 SOVL/BOVL path, W_nFAULT wire-OR, C19 with BKF = 0, and the TIM1 BRK polarity chain (BKP = 0, BKCMPxP = 0,
  POL = 0) are consistent.  With the comparators disabled their output is low, so the break is held while U2 sleeps, as
  intended.
* The VBAT_SNS divider still reads a compute-board reset (R33 into a ~50 k pull-down) as a ~0.9 V step, filtered to
  ~1.1 V/ms, below the 5 V/ms threshold.

**Verdict: 0 BLOCKER, 1 MAJOR, 3 MINOR, 3 NOTE.**  No hardware change is needed.  The main correction is to use the
DRV8316's CTRL4.DRV_OFF bit as the per-drive coast instead of the 6x-mode switch.
